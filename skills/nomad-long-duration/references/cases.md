# Nomad — cases (joint attribution)

Ranges where secondary compilations vary; page-verify before exact quotation.

## Illustrations

1. **Costco — prototype, 2002–2014+.** Bought ~$30 down from ~$55 (2000 high)
   on a basis-point margin dip + distribution build (14→40 UK capacity).
   Thesis: honest fee-for-loyalty, fixed markup, scale returned, retail
   perpetual motion, 284 → ~1,000 US runway, self-funded ~14% growth. 3.1%
   (2002) → ~10% (2004) → largest holding. Held through "expensive 24×"
   criticism via margin-reframe and the Sam's chasm analysis.
2. **Amazon — Costco playbook online, mid-2000s build, hold through doubt.**
   Bezos met 1997; the answer to "what are you doing that nobody else can do"
   arrived years later: operating + capital-cost advantage, price-giveback as
   long-lived investment absent from statements, Prime 2005 as "Costco on
   speed." ~1/6 of fund 2007; volatility "does not bother Zak and me one jot";
   sought a limit rise (quarter redeemed). 2008 halving used to upgrade into
   Amazon/Costco/Berkshire. ~2.9M shares (~$1.2B) at close per filing-based
   secondary — range only. Recommended hold for 10 years at close.
3. **Berkshire Hathaway — 10-year anchor, gratitude close.** Held ~2004–14;
   sold to return cash. Jun 2014 joint letter: not stock-picking genius but
   compounding of Berkshire people; capitalism working well.
4. **Stagecoach — repair cigar-butt to compounder lesson.** 8.6% (2003) UK bus
   debt crash; founder-operator return; sold ~90p vs 14p low (~6×). Banked
   lesson funded the later Amazon hold; 2007 attributes large gains to 2003–04
   errors studied honestly.

## Mistakes admitted (teaching material)

5. **Conseco / MBIA / Fleetwood.** Conseco Ch.11 Dec 2002 retained
   near-worthless ("mea-culpa," "mistakes inevitable, desirable, no
   window-dressing"); MBIA quick reversal without fear of rebuke; Fleetwood
   paired as same-industry error. Used to avoid US banks in 2007 (NOM-P03).
6. **Zimbabwe basket — limits of cheap.** 2004 four near-monopolies incl.
   cement at ~1/70th replacement; 2008 settlement freeze, under-capitalised
   brokers, directors marked to zero vs 0.28% prior month. "Don't think worth
   nought but cannot give anything." Revisited Dec 2013 as hard lesson.
7. **2008–09 behavior — inaction as action.** NAV ugly, price-to-value
   improved, returns deferred. Net redemptions <2% — praised as moat. Reopened
   end-2008: Amazon "priced as if it would not grow" plus similarly cheap
   names. (Emergency-meeting lore is secondary — flagged.)
8. **Closing at scale — give back, X-amount.** Returned capital rather than
   gathering billions after strong 2012–13 (secondary figures — flagged).
   Postamble: blunt regulation, rinse-repeat exhaustion, independence +
   philanthropy. Preamble: "investors don't have to change holdings with
   underwear… make money sitting on assets." Private/charity Amazon/Costco
   held toward "20% for 20 years."

## Counterexamples

- Scale captured, not shared (fails NOM-P01 despite growth).
- High margins with weak reciprocation (fails NOM-P02).
- "Long-term hold" without destination analysis (fails NOM-P03).
