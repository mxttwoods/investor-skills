# Buffett — cases

Each case: dates, what it illustrates, and the lesson's limits. Holdings are
dated disclosures, never current recommendations.

## Positive precedents

1. **See's Candies, Jan 1972 (~$25M via Blue Chip).** Seller held at $30M;
   Buffett paid $25M. Small capital base, pricing ~10%/yr without volume loss,
   cumulative pre-tax in the billions on ~$30–40M added capital (per later
   letters). Illustrates BUF-P03: pricing power + low capital needs as the moat
   prototype and the Munger-led shift to quality. Limit: tiny absolute scale.
2. **Coca-Cola, 1988–89 (~$1B for ~7%).** Post-New-Coke and post-1987-crash
   purchase of a global franchise under Goizueta/Keough; held as business growth
   plus repricing compounded (1989 letter warns the double-dip is
   non-repeatable). Illustrates BUF-P01/P03/P08.
3. **BNSF, Nov 2009 ($100/share; ~$26B for 77.4% + debt), closed Feb 2010.**
   Largest deal then; regulated, capital-intensive but efficient network moat;
   "all-in wager" deploying cash when listed equities offered little.
   Illustrates circle extension by understanding, not by price optics.
4. **Apple, from Q1 2016 (~$1B starter) to core holding, partly trimmed
   2023–24.** Understood as consumer-franchise behavior (stickiness, pricing),
   not technology forecasting. Illustrates BUF-P02 done right late — and the
   tension with "avoid fast change."

## Mistakes, reversals, misses

5. **Berkshire textile, 1962–65 to closure 1985.** Bought a terrible business on
   price reflex (tender dispute lore); fought it ~20 years. Buffett's "dumbest
   stock" admission; illustrates sunk-cost discipline and cigar-butt limits
   (BUF-P03's negative).
6. **Dexter Shoe, 1993 (~$433–443M in Berkshire shares).** Praised, then crushed
   by imports; value went to ~zero — and was paid for with irreplaceable
   compounding currency. Illustrates moat failure via foreign cost + never pay
   with crown-jewel equity (BUF-P03/P07).
7. **USAir preferred 1989 → dividend suspended 1994 → lucky exit 1998; then
   Big-4 airlines sold May 2020 (~$4B then).** Same lesson twice: commodity
   economics + leverage + demand shocks. "The world changed… my mistake"
   (May 2020 meeting). Illustrates reversal discipline (BUF-P01/P05).
8. **IBM 2011–18 (exit May 2018).** Roadmap/buyback thesis; missed the cloud
   shift; flat vs S&P strongly up. Admitted misread; contrasted with missing
   Google/Amazon ("too dumb," underestimated Bezos). Illustrates BUF-P02 breach.
9. **ConocoPhillips timing / Irish banks (2008-era admissions).** Buying into
   strength near energy/complacency peaks cost billions. Illustrates BUF-P05's
   cost when sentiment evidence is skipped.

## Counterexamples to keep in the room

- Wonderful business at an excessive price (fails BUF-P05 despite passing P03).
- Cheap cigar-butt with deteriorating economics (fails P03 despite low multiple).
- Fast-change "moat" that reprices in one product cycle (fails P02/P03).
