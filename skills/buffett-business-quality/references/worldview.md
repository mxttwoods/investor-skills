# Buffett — worldview

**Interpretation** synthesized from the sources in `sources.md`. Biographical
material is kept only where it explains the decision process.

## Formative experiences and transitions

- Omaha 1930; father Howard (congressman/stockbroker) as ethics model; early
  thrift and independence (door-to-door sales as a child). **Interpretation** from
  biographical record, not an investment rule.
- Read Graham's *The Intelligent Investor* (1949); studied under Graham and Dodd
  at Columbia (M.S. 1951); worked at Graham-Newman (1954–56). Deep-value,
  cigar-butt, and arbitrage/workout roots.
- Ran Buffett Partnerships (1956–69); took control of Berkshire Hathaway (1965).
- Met Charlie Munger (1959); Munger and Fisher's influence shifted him from
  cigar-butts to "wonderful businesses at fair prices." See's Candies (1972) is
  the documented turning point. **Documented principle** context: 1987 letter.
- Insurance (National Indemnity 1967) supplied float and permanent capital;
  later regulated capital-heavy commitments (MidAmerican, BNSF 2009–10) and the
  Apple position (from 2016) extended the circle rather than replacing it.

## Mentors and influences

- Graham/Dodd: Mr. Market, margin of safety, intrinsic value.
- Munger/Fisher: quality, pricing power, management caliber.
- GEICO's low-cost moat as the canonical castle-and-moat example (1986 letter).

## Stated values and habits

- Partnership over corporation; owner-partner attitude; eat own cooking;
  candor with look-through earnings (Owner's Manual, 1996). **Documented principle.**
- Integrity first among integrity/intelligence/energy in hiring (Florida 1998).
- Reading-centered: years of annual reports to test promises vs results; ignore
  Wall Street reports; no central meetings/budgets at Berkshire; simple tailored
  incentives (e.g. GEICO 1996: two variables). **Documented principle** for the
  incentive point; reading-volume anecdotes vary by retelling — not encoded.
- Acquisition screen (1986 letter): stated size floor, consistent earning power,
  high ROE with low debt, management in place, simple business, named price,
  quick answer, no unfriendly deals. **Documented principle.**

## Attitudes

- Uncertainty: no prediction of market direction; epidemics of fear/greed are
  unpredictable; goal is to respond, not forecast (1986). **Documented principle.**
- Money/loss: reputation over money (Salomon 1991 remarks, paraphrased);
  welcome lower prices in businesses being accumulated; tolerate lumpy results
  (prefer 15% lumpy to 12% smooth). **Documented principle.**
- Taxes favor long holding (Rip Van Winkle math, 1989). **Documented principle.**

## Evolution and tensions

- Book-value proxy → intrinsic/look-through/owner earnings measurement.
- "Avoid fast change" vs Apple; "never sell permanent holdings" (1987, qualified)
  vs later trims; price discipline vs size pressure moving the needle; trust plus
  decentralization vs reserve/underwriting control (admitted multi-year reserve
  errors, 1986). Recorded as tensions, not resolved doctrines.

## Non-replicable advantages

Insurance float (often negative-cost), no redemptions, control + tax
consolidation, capacity to write very large cat lines, reputation deal flow,
tolerance for quarterly-ugly results. None of these transfer to the agent or an
ordinary investor; the skill's procedures do not assume them.
