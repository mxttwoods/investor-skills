# Marks — operational principles

Labels: **Documented principle** / **Interpretation** / **Implementation choice**.

## MRK-P01 — Risk is permanent loss, not volatility; forward and judgment-based

- **Documented principle** (MRK-01, MRK-02): volatility is machinable for models
  but investors are paid for bearing loss/shortfall risk; risk exists only in
  the future, cannot be measured even after the fact, must be estimated by
  experienced judgment; hidden, deceptive, multi-faceted.
- Procedure: (1) name the permanent-loss paths (operating, financial,
  dilution, regulatory, behavioral); (2) estimate subjectively with ranges,
  never point precision; (3) distinguish fluctuation (tolerable) from impairment.
- Misapplications: Sharpe/volatility screens as "risk control."

## MRK-P02 — Second-level thinking: different and better

- **Documented principle** (MRK-03): outperformance needs expectations and
  portfolio divergent from consensus *and* more correct. First-level: "good
  company, buy." Second-level: what does consensus think, what is priced in,
  what if each side is right, is psychology too bullish/bearish?
- Procedure: write consensus in one paragraph; state priced-in expectations;
  argue the strongest opposing case before concluding.
- Caveat (**Documented principle**): contrarianism ≠ always diverge (2023).

## MRK-P03 — Cycles are excesses and corrections, caused by prior events

- **Documented principle** (MRK-04): not regular up/down alternation — each
  event causes the next (aversion → quality → low defaults → complacency →
  low quality → defaults → aversion). Psychology swings flawless↔hopeless while
  fundamentals move pretty-good↔not-so-hot. Psychology, credit, price, low
  return, and high risk coincide at tops (inverse at bottoms).
- Procedure: trace the causal chain backward before projecting forward.

## MRK-P04 — Price vs value: valuation drives long-run return

- **Documented principle** (MRK-05): value = present + future earning power;
  price = daily consensus vote; optimism pushes price>value and pessimism
  price<value, creating the bargains EMH denies; value's magnetic pull has
  unknowable timing.
- Procedure: state value range, current price position, and the convergence
  risks; high valuation presages low subsequent return *in the long run only*.

## MRK-P05 — Forecasting limits: important but not knowable

- **Documented principle** (MRK-06): economies have hundreds of millions of
  participants and billions of nodes; relationships are non-stationary
  (Phillips-curve failure); reflexivity + randomness dominate; models need
  simplifying assumptions that fail at inflection points — exactly when accuracy
  would pay. Extrapolation is usually right but unprofitable (priced in);
  deviation forecasts are rarely right. Join the "I-don't-know" school.
- Procedure: (1) refuse single-scenario chains (A→B→C→D→E compounding error);
  (2) require scenarios with likelihoods and E-consequences each;
  (3) publish no macro point forecast.

## MRK-P06 — Offense/defense: know where we are; move only at extremes

- **Documented principle** (MRK-07): hold normal posture (defensively biased,
  fully invested) unless temperature is compelling; "five times in 50 years"
  posture tilts; never raise cash to time; never "cheap today, cheaper in six
  months, wait."
- Procedure: temperature checklist (pattern recognition, excess/correction,
  herd extremes, own-emotion resistance, illogical-proposition test); tilt
  aggressiveness/defensiveness, never in/out.

## MRK-P07 — Avoid losers first; bear risk intelligently

- **Documented principle** (MRK-08): "If we avoid losers, winners take care of
  themselves" (since 1995; Graham/Dodd "negative art"); but aspirational
  returns also need winners. Bear only aware, analyzable, diversifiable,
  well-paid risk (life-insurance analogy).
- Procedure: downside first (how much lost, how bad must it get?); then
  asymmetry (bell-curve skew, not point upside).

## MRK-P08 — Fish in inefficient ponds; specialize; assume neutrally

- **Documented principle** (MRK-09): edge from superior inference + hard work in
  inefficient markets, not from having data; one fund = one specialty, no style
  drift; never assume better-than-normal to make an investment work; bottom-up
  with portfolio construction as defense.
- **Implementation choice**: the mandate's universe/constraints define which
  ponds are fishable here.
