# Munger — operational principles

Labels: **Documented principle** / **Interpretation** / **Implementation choice**.

## MUN-P01 — Hang facts on a latticework of multiple models

- **Documented principle** (MUN-01, MUN-02): isolated facts are unusable unless
  hung on theories from multiple disciplines; with 1–2 models, reality gets
  tortured to fit.
- Procedure: before verdict, run the thesis through math/accounting,
  microeconomics/moat, psychology, and base-rate history. One-model theses stop.
- Invalidation: any load-bearing claim with no model behind it.
- Misapplications: model-name-dropping without changing the conclusion's odds.

## MUN-P02 — Invert: solve backward

- **Documented principle** (MUN-02, crediting Jacobi): hard problems are often
  easier reversed — ask what guarantees failure/misery, then avoid it.
- Procedure: (1) list ruin paths for holders here (leverage, fraud, dilution,
  obsolescence, regulation, cost disadvantage); (2) evidence each is designed
  out; (3) un-designed-out ruin = stop.
- Investor form: "how do holders in this situation typically go broke?"

## MUN-P03 — Incentives govern: get them right, starting with your own

- **Documented principle** (MUN-03): incentive-caused bias including self and
  trusted advisors; most important management rule; FedEx night-shift example
  (pay by hour vs by job).
- Procedure: map management, seller, advisor, and analyst incentives; state
  your own (fees, career, storytelling). If incentives predict the behavior you
  bet against, downgrade.
- Misapplications: cynicism as analysis; ignoring well-designed incentives that
  support the thesis.

## MUN-P04 — Circle of competence + too-hard pile

- **Documented principle** (MUN-01, MUN-04): every person has a circle; play
  where there is edge or lose; hard problems go in the too-hard pile and stay.
- Procedure: name the edge in one sentence with filed evidence; else too-hard.
  Widen slowly by work, never by temptation.
- Output `too-hard` is success, not failure.

## MUN-P05 — Think in odds: probability, decision trees, pari-mutuel market

- **Documented principle** (MUN-01): elementary probability must be routine;
  Buffett thinks in decision trees; the market is a low-friction pari-mutuel
  where disciplined few can beat the average.
- **Interpretation** (not Munger's terms): use base rates and explicit trees;
  never label them his vocabulary.
- Procedure: (1) base rate for this situation; (2) tree with payoffs;
  (3) what the price implies; (4) where this thesis differs and why.
- Invalidation: no base rate exists and no estimate discipline is possible.

## MUN-P06 — Few big bets, concentration, long hold; avoid stupidity first

- **Documented principle** (MUN-01, MUN-05): bet seldom and heavily when odds
  favor; being consistently not-stupid beats trying to be brilliant; sit-on-ass
  patience with tax efficiency.
- **Implementation choice**: sizing caps and review cadence come from the
  mandate, not from Munger.
- Misapplications: concentration without the odds work of P05.

## MUN-P07 — Temperament: no envy, no self-pity, no ideology; be reliable

- **Documented principle** (MUN-02, MUN-06): envy/resentment/revenge/self-pity
  are disastrous; ideology cabbages the mind; sloth/unreliability fail.
- Procedure: pre-decision screen — am I hurrying, envying, story-telling, or
  defending an identity? If yes, pause and re-evidence.
- Iron prescription (**Documented principle**, 2007): cannot hold an opinion
  unless the opposing case can be stated better than its holders state it.

## MUN-P08 — Checklist + disconfirmation + lollapalooza awareness

- **Documented principle** (MUN-02, MUN-03): Darwin's disconfirmation habit;
  checklist routines (no other procedure works as well); ~two dozen tendencies
  interact multiplicatively (lollapalooza).
- Procedure: (1) list rational factors; (2) list psychological failure modes;
  (3) require checklist pass before action; (4) actively seek the best
  disconfirming evidence for the cherished theory.
- Evaluation: a pass without a disconfirmation hunt is a fail.
