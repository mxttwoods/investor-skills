# Munger — cases

Dated, inspectable precedents. Attendee-note cases are labeled as such.

## Illustrations

1. **See's Candies, 1972.** Pricing ~10%/yr without volume loss on little new
   capital — the quality-over-cigar-butt lesson Munger pressed on Buffett.
2. **Costco, 1997–2023.** Munger joined the board (Buffett declined, suggested
   him); ~25 years of "stick to knitting"; personal holding never sold while
   Berkshire exited in 2020. Find holds, not exits (2019 Daily Journal remark).
3. **BYD, 2008 (~$230M for ~10% via Li Lu).** Munger championed founder Wang
   Chuanfu (Edison-meets-Welch framing, 2009); later called it among the best
   things he helped Berkshire do — while admitting he tried to talk Wang out of
   autos and was ignored. Network + founder bet with luck owned, not hidden.
4. **Daily Journal portfolio, 2009–2023.** Post-crisis deployment into banks/BYD
   stub, then near-decade of untouched share counts: few bets, rarely sell,
   resist fiddling.

## Mistakes and reversals

5. **Alibaba, 2021–23 (Daily Journal).** Built to ~30% of the portfolio, then
   halved after souring. 2023 admission: charmed by the internet position,
   missed it was "still a goddamn retailer" in competitive e-commerce, and
   missed the regulatory risk after Jack Ma confronted regulators. Owned as one
   of his worst mistakes. Posthumous rebound does not revise the decision error.
6. **Omissions / too-hard pile: Intel at inception, Apple until 2016, retail.**
   Sat on a board with Intel's founder and passed for lack of 5-year
   understanding (2006); Apple bought only once the consumer franchise was
   legible; Buffett's early Costco refusal as retail aversion. Too-hard used
   correctly — and its cost recorded.
7. **Valeant/Sequoia, 2016 (vicarious).** "A sewer"; enormously flawed model
   enabled by incentives — used as an incentive-failure exhibit, not a holding.

## Counterexamples

- A persuasive founder story with perverse incentives underneath (fails MUN-P03).
- A "cheap" statistical bargain inside a too-hard domain (fails MUN-P04).
- A concentrated bet with no base-rate or tree work (fails MUN-P05/P06).
