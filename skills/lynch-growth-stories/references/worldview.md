# Lynch — worldview

**Interpretation** from `sources.md`.

## Formative

- Born 1944, Newton MA; father died when young; caddied from age 11 (Brae Burn),
  including for Fidelity's president; Fidelity summer intern 1966; Boston
  College 1965; Army lieutenant; Wharton MBA 1968.
- Fidelity permanent 1969 (textiles/metals/mining/chemicals); Director of
  Research 1974–77; Magellan manager May 1977–May 1990, ages 33–46.
- First tenbagger lore: Flying Tiger Airlines bought from caddy savings, sold
  multiples higher on Vietnam airlift demand — luck owned as luck.
- Says practitioners and logic/syllogism taught more than MBA quant.

## Record (reported; verify from filings before exact quotation)

- Commonly reported: ~$18–20M (1977) → ~$14B (1990); ~29%/yr vs ~15–16% S&P;
  beat the index 11 of 13 years. Largest fund by 1983. Treat yearly series as
  unverified here.

## Values and habits

- Optimistic about American business compounding; fear/greed rotate (nuclear,
  oil, Japan, LDC debt) — always ends "depression coming"; ignore it.
- Facts over forecasts: a company behind every stock; study unit-level facts
  (used-car prices, ethylene, occupancy, inventories), not macro calls.
- Workload tension honestly kept: "turn over most rocks" (look at 10, find 1)
  and hundreds of executive talks a year vs advice to know 3–5 companies cold
  on ~1 hr/week. The amateur edge is real; the workload was not amateur.
- Charity (book profits), mentoring analysts, Fidelity vice-chair after 1990.

## Attitudes

- Volatility: corrections normal and frequent; far more money lost preparing
  for corrections than in them. Stomach over brain.
- Loss: dollars, not share-price optics ("$3 — how much can I lose?" fallacy);
  never bottom-fish insolvency.

## Non-replicable advantages

Small-start freedom (1940 Act limits only), Fidelity company access and
research-director network, 1982–90 bull + IRA flows + thin mutual-fund press,
small-cap neglect era, 8,000-name opportunity set. Skill encodes the tests, not
the access.
